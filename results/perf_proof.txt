NOTE on --touch: --touch makes the benchmark harness itself write
and read every byte of the payload, on both the producer and
consumer side, by design (it's what makes the throughput/latency
numbers comparable across transports that otherwise wouldn't touch
the data at all). That means both profiles below will legitimately
show time in the harness's own fill/verify loop for every
transport, including zcring. That is expected and is NOT the
thing being tested. The question this profile answers is narrower:
does the TRANSPORT (zc_reserve/zc_commit/zc_acquire/zc_release for
zcring; write(2)/read(2) for unix) additionally copy the payload on
top of the harness's own touch -- i.e. does a copy symbol
(copy_user_generic, __memmove_*, memcpy, etc) appear attributable
to the transport path, not to the harness's touch loop.

Two things to know before reading the tables below as "missing ==
innocent":
  1. zc_reserve/zc_commit/zc_acquire/zc_release are 'static inline'
     in src/zcring.h, so at -O2 they are inlined into main() rather
     than appearing as their own symbols. Their cost (what there is
     of it) is folded into the 'main' row, not absent from the
     profile. Confirmed by grep: 'static inline' on all four.
  2. clock_gettime()/[vdso] entries are the harness's own latency
     timestamping (one call per message on each side), not the
     transport. They show up in both profiles at similar weight and
     are not part of what this proof is checking.
